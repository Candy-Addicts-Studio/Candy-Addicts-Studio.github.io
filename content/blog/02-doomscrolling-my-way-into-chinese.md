## Why Should I Doomscroll In Chinese Rather Than Study Chinese?

That's a great question. And I wouldn't know how to answer which one is more effective. But one sure is easier to stick
to.

Not so early into my Mandarin journey, I had the idea of trying to consume media in Chinese. At the time, I had mostly
consumed just study materials, which were pretty dull, and not motivating enough for my dopamine-addicted millenial
brain. I wanted to stop studying and do something else, but I had the mental weight of my consciousness telling me that
I was just slacking off my studies (it was right).

Then, the idea came to me: to try the Chinese version of TikTok. The videos are short, often paired with subtitles, and
there's a lot of day-to-day vocabulary. Plus, the visual context of the video helps you infer the words you don't yet
know. And if you absolutely can't understand what's going on, just swipe up and try again on a new video.

## Did Watching Douyin help my Chinese?

Yes. But at the start, I could only get the simplest phrases, like "Hi auntie!" and so on. Since I never dropped the
formal studies, they helped me reach a point where I could understand more to be able to go on by myself. Reaching that
point, I could watch most videos with having to look up just one or two words on Pleco to understand it.

By this point, I still hadn't bought the pleco OCR tool, which would've made this process faster. But I was learning at
a pace I felt good about, so it didn't matter. I got faster at reading and better at listening, so I do recommend this a
lot if you're past the beginner step of learning chinese.

## What's After Doomscrolling?

After a while, I got comfortable with consuming Douyin. It was around the time I bought a new, better phone, so I got
really curious to try something different: gaming.

If you have ever heard about what are the most popular mobile games, you wouldn't be surprised that I chose to play a
Mihoyo game. The gacha I chose was Honkai: Star Rail, a turn-based combat game with lots of dialogue (as per the Mihoyo
standard). Being Chinese, it has an option for complete Chinese text and Mandarin audio.

This was a very big step out of my comfort zone. Video content on Douyin always came in the short form, but the game has
a storyline that spans tens of hours of gameplay for each arc. The errors definetly have more room to compound and byte
you back (which leads to funny situations like "what do you mean these are the baddies? I thought they were the good
guys!").

## Gaming In Chinese With The Right Tools

By this point I was a bit overwhelmed, but still motivated to go on: the game was nice, and I wanted to see more of the
story (the character interactions are way too fun). But the walls of text were getting to me. Swapping apps going
to/from the game and Pleco was killing me. So I decided to buy Pleco's OCR extension.

It saved me a lot of time, and most of the time it got the right character. The only downside is that it was slow to
start and it froze the screen when you clicked the "scan" button. That made it usable for Honkai dialogue, but a pain to
pair with Douyin if I wanted to, since clicking scan would freeze my screen but not pause the video, which would go on
playing.

## How To Use Pleco OCR On Video (or Game) Content Then?

If you're on Android and want a solution to watching Chinese content to learn the language, Pleco's OCR extension will
definetly help, but I still wouldn't call it the right tool yet. My frustration with this specific use-case led me to
develop [my own Chinese reading app](/overlayocr/), tailored for this end.

It doesn't have a "scan" button: it continually scans a selected screen area for changes, and decoretes detected text
with an underline. When clicked, it reveals a dialogue with the word definition, tones and pinyin. Optionally, it can
automatically pause whatever media you're watching while you read this unknown word, and automatically resume once
you're finished.

For the gaming use case, you position it over the dialogue area and hide the boundaries. So everytime a dialogue comes
up, it gets underlined and becomes a clickable that you can interact to look up the meaning. You also don't need to give
sensitive permissions to the app, like the accessibility one required by pleco. When scanning, you select only a single
app to share the screen, so it doens't see what it doesn't need to.
